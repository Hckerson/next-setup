# Registers research: PLAYFUL AND BOLD, second in-product exemplar (Headspace)

Method. Same as `references/registers/trust-playful.md`. I downloaded the shipped stylesheets with curl, read them for custom properties and computed values, and resolved every `var()` chain to its token:

- **my.headspace.com (the logged-in web app):** the shell HTML links one stylesheet, `https://my.headspace.com/static/js/index-CrU0nYfW.css`. It is 186,293 bytes of Panda CSS (`--made-with-panda`), with 873 custom-property definitions and 667 unique. Its components are Panda recipes (`.button--variant_primary`, `.choiceOption`, `.playDot`, `.timelineStep`, `.screenHero` and others), and a `.dark` class redefines the semantic roles.
- **App JS chunks**, same origin under `/static/js/`: `siteNavLayoutStyles-Beot4XKj.js` and `siteNavRailGapStyles-CmNg_ip0.js` (rail geometry), `SiteNavigationShellSkeleton-CFNhuYeQ.js` (nav item states), `SearchEmptyState-8t0Bu7fk.js` (empty-state illustration and copy) and `index-mzncoF3t.js` (entry).
- **www.headspace.com (marketing):** a 556 KB SSR page carrying 148,718 bytes of inline style. It was checked only to confirm it uses the same palette and face.

App screens were read from Mobbin (ids given). I computed contrast with WCAG relative luminance, flattening alpha onto the stated surface, and computed OKLCH from sRGB myself. Tags: **Q** = QUALITY (register-independent), **R** = REGISTER (a choice another premium register would legitimately make differently). Anything not measured is marked UNVERIFIED.

Caveats:

- The web app is real product CSS, not marketing. But the session content (players, Sleep, the Today timeline) is server-driven (`sduiRoutes`, `flutterHostRoutes`), so some screens render through runtime styles that are not in the stylesheet. iOS behaviour comes from Mobbin previews, which are scaled, so pixel sizes read from them are approximate and marked UNVERIFIED.
- The Panda preset ships the full Tailwind-style palettes (slate, gray, zinc, neutral, stone, mauve, olive, mist, taupe and the hue ramps at 50 and 500 to 950). These are framework defaults. Only the Headspace ramps (hex values at 100 to 400, plus `warm-grey`, `cool-grey` and `winkle`) are referenced by the semantic roles, and only those are treated as Headspace's palette below.

---

## Choice: Headspace over Arc

Headspace is the stronger in-product exemplar. Its logged-in web app ships a complete, measurable semantic token layer and component recipes. Mobbin has broad coverage of the iOS product (Today, Explore, players, Sleep, profile, sheets, onboarding). It is also a consensus top-tier design product: Apple Design Award 2023, Social Impact, cited for "signature illustrations" (https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/06/apple-announces-winners-of-the-2023-apple-design-awards/), and two 2023 Webbys, Best User Interface and Best Visual Design – Function (https://winners.webbyawards.com/2023/apps-software/app-features/best-user-interface/256532/headspace).

Arc is weaker on every count:

- Its playful product surface is a native desktop app with no shipped CSS to measure.
- Mobbin carries only Arc Search (iOS), whose chrome is mostly stock glass sheets and grouped lists (Mobbin `fdab8332-20f7-4229-a9fa-67bd412bf2c9`, `f765ff38-7958-44a1-93b4-b44557213d14`).
- arc.net's stylesheet (`/_next/static/css/6d65f8dcbcc1dcc5.css`, 48,979 bytes) has only 55 unique custom properties, and its font sizes, radii and shadows are ad hoc (24 distinct font sizes, 18 radii).
- Arc went into maintenance mode in May 2025 and is announced to stop working on 22 December 2026 (https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/10/23/arc-browser-on-its-way-out-as-makers-suggest-an-alternative/5305114). An exemplar that disappears in six weeks cannot anchor a register.

## Point of view

**A calm toy.** The frame is a quiet, warm, soft tool: white ground, warm-grey tonal cards with no borders, pill controls, one blue for every action and selection. The play is carried by the content: full-bleed colour fields, horizon arcs, and closed-eyed faces whose expression sets the mood of each piece. Bold colour is spent on what you listen to, never on the chrome.

Headspace states the intent itself: "flex from playful to clinical", "bright, uplifting, bold and lively", and faces that express "not just joy — but stress, sadness, contentment, and every mood in between" (It's Nice That, Liz Gorny, 25 Apr 2024, https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424).

Where Duolingo is a toy that rewards each tap, Headspace is a toy that sets a mood.

## Per-layer realisation notes

- **01 Token.** Roles are layered over named ramps (100 to 800), and a role name says its job (`interactive-stronger`, `surface-on-modal`, `foreground-highlight`). Two neutral families exist, not one: warm for day and navy for night. `static` roles are the device that lets content own its colour in both themes, and `contrast-*` is an inverted surface for transient UI.
- **02 Scale class.** One family carries every role. Headings and labels are tracked at a constant −0.03em, body at 0. A mono uppercase utility role labels structure ("PART 1 · SESSION 1", Mobbin `99e475d3-6e41-4266-aad1-8a2f1e781f2d`). Each role has a `base` and a `large` lock, so the scale is responsive by role, not by breakpoint utilities.
- **03 Primitive.** Pill buttons in five sizes (24 to 56). The single lip marks the primary action and disappears on press. Focus is a 2px blue outline at 4px offset. Static and overlay variants exist so controls can sit on illustration. Fix the hover-equals-press gap before copying.
- **05 Pattern.** The horizon (`screenHero` ellipse) ends every hero. The Play Dot is the one oversized control. The Today timeline is the progress pattern.
- **06 Mark.** Status reads by shape and size first (check, larger dot, outline) and colour second. Stats are illustrated glyphs plus counts.
- **07 Row.** A card per row with a tonal fill and no border, image-led. History rows group by date along a rule.
- **08 List and modal.** Sheets and dialogs at radius 24, enter via `@starting-style`, with reduced motion honoured. Destructive actions are red text in sheet rows, not red buttons.
- **09 Section.** Colour is spent per piece of content (playlist pairs, full-bleed players), never per chrome region.
- **12 Chrome.** Quiet: a hairline rail, monochrome glyphs, regular-weight labels and a warm pill for selection. On mobile web a floating blurred pill bar; on iOS three tabs.
- **13 and 14 Layout.** Server-driven columns (`--sdui-column: calc(clamp(25.875rem, 62.35vw − 4.0625rem, 35.8125rem) + 3rem)`; `--content-info-grid: min(100vw − 6rem, 84rem)`). The rail's left gap is computed fluidly (`siteNavRailGapStyles`).

## Failure mode (the AI tell for this instance)

Orange smiley blobs on every card, peach and lavender gradients, everything pill-shaped, a beige "wellness" ground, and a script or rounded display face. It copies the costume and drops the rules:

- faces that never change expression, so the character carries no state;
- colour without a domain map (Sleep is a mode on the navy ladder, not a purple accent);
- playful illustrations dropped into a template SaaS shell with a loud sidebar;
- brand orange used as text (2.73:1);
- hover and press made identical, or the lip put on every card instead of on the one primary action.

## Quality gaps in the exemplar (do not copy)

- **Orange as text and state.** `foreground-highlight` and `status-highlight` are orange `#ff7300` at 2.73:1 on white, below 3:1 even for large text and for non-text UI. The active timeline dot (20px orange) passes only because size also distinguishes it. Usage of `.text--color_foregroundHighlight` in screens is UNVERIFIED.
- **Control boundaries.** The input and select border `#e2ded9` is 1.34:1. Choice tiles are `#f9f4f2` on white, 1.09:1. The area of the control is near-invisible on a white ground (label text alone identifies it).
- **Hover equals press.** Secondary, tertiary, ghost, static-white and static-black buttons use one value for both. Primary distinguishes press only by removing a 2px 20% lip.
- **Disabled by opacity, inconsistently.** Most components use .4, the toggle .48, the bottom nav .38. There is no designed disabled pair (Duolingo's swan fill plus hare text is the better instance).
- **Token pipeline leaks.** `--spacing-smallX` is used by `.badge [data-part=dot]` but never defined, so the dot collapses. `.bottomNavigation [data-part=nav-label]{font-size:xSmall}` is invalid, so the label falls back to an inherited size. Six utilities ship raw token names (`padding: small medium`, `padding: 0 large large`, `margin: small 0`) that browsers drop.
- **Ramp name collision.** Headspace hex values occupy `red-100…400`, `blue-100…400` and the others, inside the preset's OKLCH ramps (`red-50`, `red-500…950`). One ramp name mixes two systems. About 240 preset colour properties ship unused in `:root`.
- **Dark focus margin.** The blue ring is 3.33:1 on the dark modal surface: a pass, but a thin one.

The exemplar fixes Duolingo's gaps: action contrast 5.31, a focus system, and reduced-motion handling. A playful build can therefore cite one in-product exemplar that meets the accessibility bar.

## Statements in `registers/playful.md` (and the README row) this exemplar contradicts

1. **Point of view, "chunky physical controls and characters reward every action".** Headspace controls are 40 to 56px pills with one 2px neutral lip, and its characters set the content's mood without reacting to taps (tap reaction UNVERIFIED on iOS). The register needs a second reading: characters may carry the content's emotional state instead of rewarding actions.
2. **Point of view, "keep type and chrome black and white so the colour can shout".** Headspace's chrome is warm grey with a blue action, not black and white. The principle (chrome quiet so colour can shout) holds; the instance does not.
3. **Neutral axis position, "Pure in light themes, tinted in dark"** (also README "Pure in light, tinted in dark"). Headspace's light neutrals are warm-tinted (C up to 0.012, H 68 to 85). Its dark is a strong navy (C 0.06 to 0.08), not a faint tint.
4. **Type axis position, "Rounded heavy, often uppercase labels; or a variable family with axes as roles"** (README "Rounded heavy, or variable with axes as roles"). Headspace is a non-rounded grotesque at 400 and 700 in mixed case, with a mono utility role. The README's own menu calls this "one neutral sans with finer weights" plus a mono label, a position currently read as developer-tool.
5. **Chrome axis position, "Loud labelled sidebar with illustrated icons"; layer note "Chrome: deliberately loud, correct for a consumer habit app"; README "Loud labelled sidebar".** Headspace is a consumer habit app with quiet chrome: a hairline rail, monochrome glyphs and regular labels. Loud chrome is Duolingo's instance, not the register's.
6. **Layer note, Primitive: "Hover, press and disabled are distinct and continuous: brightness, then key travel by the lip height, then a muted fill. Press equals travel."** Headspace's press removes the lip without travel, and disabled is opacity. "Press equals travel" is Duolingo's instance; the register-level property is "press visibly changes depth or fill". (Headspace's hover-equals-press is a gap, not an alternative.)
7. **Layer note, Row and list: "One selection recipe shared by nav items, answer tiles and list items".** Headspace uses three container recipes (warm fill, darker border, ink inversion) sharing one selection colour on the indicator. The register-level property is better stated as "one selection colour".
8. **Layer note, Token: "A colour is a (fill, lip) pair".** In Headspace only the primary has a lip, and it is neutral ink, not a hue shade. The pair is Duolingo's instance.
9. **Value economy, "Expressive", observed as "131 font sizes; overshoot easings".** Headspace has no overshoot and a single dominant duration. Its UI value set is restrained, and the expressiveness is carried by imagery. Consider redefining the core as "expressive in imagery and colour fields; the UI value set may be moderate".
10. **Suits and strains, "Strains for regulated, proof-heavy … work".** Headspace runs this register into clinical mental healthcare (therapy and coaching) and chose its face to "flex from playful to clinical" (It's Nice That). The register can strain less than stated when chrome stays quiet.
11. **"Duolingo is this register's only in-product exemplar"** (Staying in the register). It is no longer true once Headspace is added. The worked example should then name Headspace's instances as a second set to steer away from (Apercu-style grotesque at −3%, pill controls in 16px cards, the warm-grey tonal card, the horizon arc, the closed-eyed face).
12. **Exemplar gaps, "Copying Duolingo's look means fixing these".** Headspace shows the register meeting the bar in-product: action contrast 5.31, a 2px outline at 4px offset, and 4 reduced-motion blocks.

Not contradicted: several hues with jobs (core); colour pairs per section with maximal schemes reserved for emphasis; status by illustrated glyph plus colour; designed dark values per colour.
