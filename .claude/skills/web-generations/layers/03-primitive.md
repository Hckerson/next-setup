# Layer 3: Primitive

`components/ui/`. Generic, domain-free controls: Button, Badge, Input, Modal base, Menu, Tooltip, Tabs, Skeleton, Toast.

**Upstream:** Token, Scale class. A recurring tell here is usually inherited from these layers: trace it there and fix it once, not once per file here.

## Tells

A tell fires on a control left at the library's or the era's default, decided by nobody. A shape, ring, height or physical device chosen on purpose and recorded in the brief is a register, not a tell.

## Floor versus baseline

Fourth-generation primitives are accessible, consistent and themeable, and they still look like the library: its radius, its heights, its ring, its overlay animation. They lack a full state set (press, busy and invalid designed, not only hover), focus designed for each surface, overlay motion that someone decided, and the project's shape language. The baseline asks for the states and the decisions; the register decides how they look and how fast they move.

## Baseline

Every register meets these. Counts are of the 18 exemplars (`references/classification-ledger.md`, section 03).

- **PRI-B1. Every state named.** Each interactive state a component has (rest, hover, press, focus-visible, selected, invalid, disabled, busy) is a distinct named value for fill, border and text. The exact list belongs to the component. `(S 9/18)` Stripe's error-hover and error-focus are its instance, not the list.
- **PRI-B2. Press differs from hover.** Press is its own change, not hover held down. `(S 3/18)` Duolingo's key travel against a brightness hover; Mercury `active:` tokens; Wise active colours.
- **PRI-B3. Hover is one small change.** Hover is one small, consistent change on one property family (a ladder step, an alpha wash or a filter), with no lift and no shadow growth. `(S 9/18)` Aesop #333 to #252525; Duolingo's brightness filter and Figma's 85 percent black pass too. Its tempo is the register's.
- **PRI-B4. Designed focus.** Every interactive element has a designed focus indicator, not the UA default, that reaches 3:1 against every colour adjacent to it on every surface it can land on, is distinct from hover, and works without JavaScript. Where a control has a hard lip or offset depth, the indicator sits outside the lip and its contrast is measured against the page, not against the lip. `(S)` Wise's 2px ink outline (19.23:1) passes with one layer. Duolingo (no focus), Aesop (focus only through JavaScript), The Pudding (1.66:1 on its light theme) and Things (about 1.99 to 2.91:1) are defects, not registers. The focus indicator differs from any resting border in colour, width or offset, so it is never drawn exactly over the border it sits on.
- **PRI-B5. Focus fits the surface.** Dense rows and menu items have an inset variant so the indicator never clips; the indicator never shifts layout; a gap layer in the surface colour is added only when one ring colour cannot reach 3:1 on every surface. `(S)` Inset: Mercury menu items, Wise list items, Linear inner items. No shift: Ableton's reserved transparent border, Aesop's overlaid highlighter. Gap: Aesop's cream gap over dark imagery.
- **PRI-B6. Busy is its own state.** Busy differs from disabled and keeps the control's width. `(S 4/18)` Wise hides the label with `visibility: hidden`; Aesop keeps the fill and adds a spinner; Mercury and Vercel ship it as a token or prop.
- **PRI-B7. Disabled is a token.** Disabled is a token (an opacity, a blend or a tonal pair), never a literal. `(S 6/18)` Apple .42; Wise .45 with a luminosity blend; Duolingo and Aesop tonal pairs.
- **PRI-B8. Emphasis crossed with tone.** Button variants are an emphasis scale (at least strong, secondary, quiet) crossed with tone (neutral, accent, danger). Product names go on actions, not on the axes: "Cancel order" is a strong danger Button with a product label. Where applicable: a danger tone is required only when the product has a destructive action. `(S 6/18)`
- **PRI-B9. State in more than colour.** Badge and mark primitives carry state in a second channel: a word, a glyph shape or a change to the content. `(S 8/18)` Vercel's dot plus "Ready", Linear's glyph shapes, Mercury's struck-through amount.
- **PRI-B10. Sizes are tokens.** Control heights come from tokens, icon-only controls carry an accessible name, and every size shipped has loading and disabled. How many sizes is the register's. `(S)` Aesop ships one height; Wise ships four.
- **PRI-B11. Touch target.** Targets are at least 44px on touch, measured at a touch viewport. `(S 6/18)` Apple, Stripe, TE, Aesop, Wise, Duolingo. Mercury marketing's 32 and 40px buttons are a gap, not a register.
- **PRI-B12. Identifying boundaries reach 3:1.** Where a boundary is what identifies a control (an input, a tile), it reaches 3:1 against its surface. A resting border that identifies nothing may be fainter. `(S 1/18, thin; see TOK-B9)` Wise's input ring at 3.64:1. Mercury and Figma resting borders at 1.13 to 1.45:1 pass only where the boundary is not needed.
- **PRI-B13. Validity designed.** A field's invalid state (and valid, where the product shows it) is designed as a state of the field. `(S 2/18, thin)` Aesop's error ring and valid border; Wise's 3px inset error ring. Label, hint and error wired as one unit with `aria-describedby` is `(J)`.
- **PRI-B14. Designed enter and exit.** Every overlay's enter and exit are designed and recorded in the brief; nothing pops by omission. An instant cut is allowed only as a recorded decision. The exit never delays the user's next action, and scrims fade with their layer. Tempo is a recorded brief decision in every register: whether exit is faster than, equal to or slower than enter, the scale and the duration are chosen in the brief. No register requires a direction, and equal durations are not a gap. `(S)` Mercury app, Vercel, Apple, Notion. TE's modal and Aesop's flyout show no measured transition: recorded cuts if confirmed, gaps otherwise.
- **PRI-B15. Exits survive reduced motion.** Where JavaScript waits for a transition to end, the reduced-motion duration stays above zero (1ms), or the end event never fires and the overlay sticks. `(S 1/18, thin)` The Pudding sets its base duration to 1ms; Nothing's `0s !important` is a risk. See TOK-B15.
- **PRI-B16. Dialog behaviour.** The base dialog traps focus, locks scroll, closes on Escape, closes on outside click for non-destructive content, returns focus to its trigger, and has a close control with an accessible name. `(S 1/18, thin)` Vercel; this is the WAI-ARIA dialog pattern, independent of style.
- **PRI-B17. Sheets on narrow touch screens.** On narrow touch screens a dialog becomes a sheet or a full-screen surface. `(S 3/18, thin)` Vercel, Things iOS, Apple Store iOS.

## Techniques

Register-neutral methods, recommended, not required.

- **Disabled says why.** Where a disabled control blocks the user, give the reason beside it, or in a tooltip on a focusable wrapper. Not measured in any exemplar, so it is not a criterion.
- **Reserving the focus edge.** A transparent border at rest that takes colour on focus keeps layout still (Ableton); so does a highlighter positioned as an overlay (Aesop). An outline never affects layout.
- **Forced colours.** When focus is drawn with a border or a shadow, add a transparent outline so it survives forced-colours mode (Nothing).
- **Reduced-motion durations.** Override the base duration to 1ms rather than 0 under reduced motion, so `transitionend` still fires (The Pudding).

## Token roles this layer needs

Control height scale and touch minimum; per-state fill, border and text (rest, hover, press, focus, selected, invalid, disabled, busy); disabled treatment; focus indicator (colour, width, offset or inset, and a gap colour where one ring colour cannot pass everywhere); control boundary at 3:1 where it identifies the control; overlay enter and exit (duration, easing, and any transform); shape values for control and overlay.
