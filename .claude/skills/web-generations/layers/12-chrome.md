# Layer 12: Chrome

`components/layout/`. The frame around the content: sidebar, topbar, header, footer, navigation, command palette, account menu.

**Upstream:** Token, Brand, Primitive, Pattern. A recurring tell here is usually inherited from these layers: trace it there and fix it once, not once per file here.

## Floor versus baseline

A competent fourth-generation chrome is the library's header, sidebar and footer, wired correctly. It lacks decisions: chrome that responds to where you are (route, scroll, section), one primary action per bar, a footer built from the site's own structure, and focus, contrast and target sizes that hold on every surface the chrome crosses. How loud, thin or dim the chrome is belongs to the register.

## Baseline

Every register meets these. Counts are of the 18 exemplars (`references/classification-ledger.md`, section 12).

- **CHR-B1. Location is marked.** The current item carries `aria-current` and a visible current state that does not rely on colour alone. `(J, thin)` Apple's local nav underlines the current link; no other exemplar was measured.
- **CHR-B2. Marketing: chrome responds to context.** The header changes material with scroll or with the section beneath it (filled, ruled, frosted or re-themed), and the change is designed. When a section swaps theme, the chrome follows with every role intact. `(S 7/18)` Aesop transparent to cream, Apple transparent to frosted, Linear, Vercel, Mercury re-themed per section, Wise transparent then fixed and recoloured, Things a scrolled state.
- **CHR-B3. One primary action per bar.** Each bar carries at most one primary action; anything else in it is visibly secondary. `(S)` Apple's local nav carries exactly one "Buy"; Mercury, Wise and Figma pair a quiet "Log in" with one filled action; Mercury's app top bar has one ("Move money").
- **CHR-B4. Glass is earned.** Glass or blur appears only on floating or stuck chrome with content passing beneath it, and has a designed no-blur fallback. `(S 2/18, thin)` Apple (alpha .8 with blur, falling back to .92 with none); Things (blur only once scrolled).
- **CHR-B5. Chrome is built from tokens.** Header, sub-header and sidebar dimensions are named tokens that layouts and scroll padding share, and any chrome rule uses the recorded border treatment (TOK-B9). `(S 4/18)` Nothing, Apple, Mercury, Wise.
- **CHR-B6. Marketing: the footer follows the site.** The footer is grouped by the site's own tasks and carries the legal line; its size follows the catalogue, from a few links to a full directory. `(S)` Aesop and Apple directories, Ableton, Teenage Engineering, Nothing.
- **CHR-B7. App: a palette is real or absent.** If a command palette ships, it has combobox semantics and an active descendant, scrolls the active row into view, shows a key hint, and covers the product's real actions. `(J, thin)` Raycast and Mercury ship palettes; their semantics were not measured.
- **CHR-B8. Chrome text reaches contrast.** Nav labels, chrome links and footer text that carry meaning reach 4.5:1 on their surface (3:1 for large text). Chrome is made quieter by size, weight and surface, never by taking essential text below that line. `(S)` Defects, not register: Things' nav at about 2.84:1, Duolingo's nav labels at 4.48:1.
- **CHR-B9. Visible designed focus.** Every chrome control has a designed focus indicator, not the UA default, that reaches 3:1 against every adjacent colour on every surface it can land on (a transparent header over imagery included), is distinct from hover, does not shift layout, works without JavaScript, and has an inset variant for dense sidebar items and menu rows. If one ring colour cannot reach 3:1 everywhere, add a gap layer in the surface colour. `(S)` Ring style is register. Defects: Duolingo removes outlines globally; Aesop's focus depends on JavaScript.
- **CHR-B10. Touch targets.** At a touch viewport every chrome control, the menu toggle and icon actions included, has a hit area of at least 44px. `(S)` Apple, Stripe, Teenage Engineering, Aesop, Wise, Duolingo. Mercury's 32 and 40px marketing buttons are a defect. Measure at a touch viewport only.
- **CHR-B11. Skip link and route focus.** Every surface has a skip link to a focusable `main`. App: on a client-side route change, focus moves to the new content. `(S 3/18)` Pudding, Ableton, Figma for the skip link; route-change focus is unmeasured (thin).
- **CHR-B12. Disclosure by click and keyboard.** Menus and flyouts open on click and from the keyboard with `aria-expanded`, never on hover alone; a drawer traps focus while open and closes on Escape, while a menu does not trap (arrow keys within, Escape and Tab close, focus returns to the toggle), as LST-B6; its enter and exit are designed, as for any overlay. `(J, thin)` Apple's hover flyouts were not tested by keyboard.

## Techniques

Register-neutral methods, recommended, not required.

- **A travelling active indicator.** An indicator that moves between items on a shared layout gives selection continuity (CHR-G4-2). Seen in production, unmeasured in the exemplars (Aesop's travelling element is a focus highlighter, not an active indicator), so it is a technique, not a criterion.

## Token roles this layer needs

Header, sub-header and sidebar dimensions; nav item height; chrome surface with its scrolled and themed states; chrome rule from the border treatment; selected-item treatment; glass material and its no-blur fallback; focus colour and its inset variant; a z-index layer for sticky chrome; a shortcut-chip type role where the register surfaces shortcuts.

## Checks

Baseline:

1. Does the nav mark the current item with `aria-current` and a visible state beyond colour? `[source]`
2. Marketing: does the header change material with scroll or section, by design, and follow a section's theme swap? `[app]`
3. Does each bar carry at most one primary action? `[source]`
4. Is glass or blur limited to floating or stuck chrome, with a no-blur fallback? `[source]`
5. Are chrome dimensions named tokens, and do chrome rules use the recorded border treatment? `[source]`
6. Marketing: is the footer grouped by the site's own tasks, with the legal line, and free of placeholder groups and `#` links? `[source]`
7. App: if a command palette ships, does it have combobox semantics, an active descendant and real actions, with no hard-coded `aria-expanded`? `[source]`
8. Does every chrome text step that carries meaning reach 4.5:1 (3:1 large) on its surface, by calculation? `[source]`
9. Does every chrome control show a designed focus at 3:1 on every surface it can land on, distinct from hover, without layout shift and without JavaScript? `[source]`, look `[app]`
10. At a touch viewport, is every chrome control at least 44px? `[app]`
11. Are there a skip link, a focusable main and route-change focus management? Search `app/` and the layout files; absence is "not found". `[source]`
12. Is hover never the only way to open a menu, does a hand-rolled drawer trap focus and close on Escape, and does a menu close on Escape and Tab without trapping? `[source]`

Register fit:

13. Do the chrome's loudness, selection treatment, header model, shortcut surface and footer size match the register the brief names, rather than a dim developer-tool sidebar by default? Where app and marketing use different registers, does each surface follow its own? `[source]`, look `[app]`
