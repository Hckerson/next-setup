# Service floor

Restaurants, shops, gyms and salons. The world's rule is the room: covers, chairs, classes and tills run on the book and the ticket, against the clock of a shift.

## Artefacts

| Artefact | Can supply |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Kitchen order tickets on the rail, the pass, the expo screen | Row anatomy (table, course, time fired), the ticket as a shape, the age of an order as a state |
| Reservation books and floor plans (tables by number, covers, turns) | A plan as navigation, table numbers as a numeric style, a time grid |
| Menus (chalkboard, printed card, a daily sheet) | Type voice from the house's own menu, price alignment, a section order |
| Appointment books (salon chairs, stylists across, times down) | A column-per-person console, a slot as a shape |
| Class timetables and punch or loyalty cards (fitness, cafes) | A weekly grid, a stamp per visit as progress |
| Shelf-edge labels and price tags, the till receipt (retail) | Price and unit price type, a label system |
| The uniform, apron, tiles, signage of the actual room | Neutrals and accent taken from the place itself |

## Standards

- Allergen labelling rules (the fourteen in the UK and EU, the nine in the US) as a status vocabulary with marks and words.
- Food hygiene and licensing displays where the jurisdiction requires them.
- Unit pricing rules for shelf labels.
- Deposit and cancellation terms shown before a booking is held.

## Archetype pressure

Console (a floor plan or a chair grid that changes through a shift), rows (tickets, bookings), marks (seated, fired, late, no-show, ready), page (a menu or a timetable read on a phone at the door).

## The category template

A full-bleed dark food photo with a script logo and "Book a table"; a salon in pastel pink with a script face and a model's hair; a gym in black and neon with a sweating athlete; three cards with Instagram photos.

## Stage notes

`daylight-location` is the real room at its service hour (the window, the pass, the chair); `miniature-world` takes the floor plan as a model room the covers move through; `macro-material` takes the food, the cloth, the steel.
