# Catalogue

Shops, showrooms, listings and auction houses. The world's rule is the lot: each object is numbered, measured, described and priced so a buyer can compare it with the next.

## Artefacts

| Artefact | Can supply |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Mail-order and trade catalogues (item numbers, swatches, dimensioned drawings) | A numbering device, a spec table, an index |
| Auction catalogues (lot number, estimate, provenance, condition) | Row anatomy, the estimate range as a numeric style, a sale-day state |
| Museum wall labels (artist, title, date, medium, credit line) | A label anatomy with a fixed order, a caption voice |
| Lookbooks and season drops | Sequence and pacing, a drop as a dated event |
| Size charts, fit guides, swatch cards | A grid device, a material accent |
| Car brochures and spec sheets | Specification tables, options as a configurator's grammar |
| Property particulars (floor plan, energy rating bars, room sizes) | A plan, a rating scale with a real source |

## Standards

- **GS1** product identifiers and item-level data; unit pricing rules.
- Energy and efficiency labels where they apply (the EPC bands for property, appliance energy labels) as scales with a real source.
- Size standards by market (EU, UK, US) shown together, never guessed.
- Museum label conventions (tombstone order, credit lines).

## Archetype pressure

Rows and lists (a grid of lots, filters), patterns (compare, gallery, zoom), page (a product or lot page that answers the buyer's questions in order), modals (a bid, a basket, a size choice).

## The category template

A hero product on a beige background with "Shop now", a four-up product grid with heart icons, a lifestyle photo with a serif headline, a newsletter pop-up with ten percent off; for property, a drone shot of a house at dusk.

## Stage notes

`studio-sweep` takes the object photographed as the catalogue would; `print-catalogue` takes the catalogue itself as the actor; `colour-field` takes the season's colourway.
