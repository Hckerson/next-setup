# Playbill

Shows, gigs, weddings, studios, services and meetups. The world's rule is the night: a bill of who is on, a ticket to get in, a running order, and the feeling after.

## Artefacts

| Artefact | Can supply |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Letterpress and wood-type posters (stacked bill, sizes by billing) | A display type scale where size is rank, a poster layout |
| Theatre programmes and orders of service | A running order as navigation, a cast or a reading list as rows |
| Tickets with stubs, wristbands, stamps on the hand | A perforation as a shape, a ticket anatomy (date, venue, row, seat) |
| Set lists, cue sheets, run-of-show documents | A time-coded row, a cue as a mark |
| Record sleeves and labels (catalogue number, side, tracklist, credits) | A credits voice, a circular label device, catalogue numbering |
| Wedding stationery (invitation, RSVP, table plan, place cards) | A gentle register: names first, a table plan as a console |
| Hymn boards and service sheets | Numbers in slots as a device, a calendar of services |

## Standards

- Ticketing fields (date, doors, venue, age limit, accessibility) shown before purchase.
- Credits conventions for music (performer, writer, producer, label, catalogue number).
- Safety and consent controls for social products (block, report, who can see) as a status vocabulary, not an afterthought.
- Loudness and flashing limits for anything that plays (no more than three flashes a second).

## Archetype pressure

Page (the bill, the event page), rows (line-ups, tracklists, guests, matches), marks (on sale, sold out, cancelled, matched, pending), modals (a ticket, an RSVP, a block).

## The category template

A concert crowd photo with hands up under a purple-to-pink gradient, neon type, a countdown timer; for weddings, a script face over florals and blush; for dating, two phones with hearts.

## Stage notes

`colour-field` takes the bill's colour; `night-luminous` is the real venue lit by its own rig; `paper-cut` takes the stationery and the poster as layers.
