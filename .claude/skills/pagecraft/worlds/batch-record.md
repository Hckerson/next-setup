# Batch record

Labs, plants that make by the batch, kitchens at scale and regulated retail. The world's rule is traceability: every lot links to a measurement, inside its limits, signed and witnessed.

## Artefacts

| Artefact | Can supply |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Bound lab notebooks (numbered pages, grid, dated entries, witness signatures) | A ground, a page number as a device, an entry anatomy (date, aim, result, signed) |
| Batch manufacturing records and certificates of analysis | Row anatomy (test, specification, result, pass), the limit as a visible rule |
| Control charts (centre line, upper and lower limits, runs) | A chart language where the limits are drawn, not implied |
| Hazard labels and safety data sheets (pictogram diamonds, sixteen numbered sections) | A diamond or plate mark confined to hazard, numbered sections as navigation |
| Lot and expiry codes, sample labels, tube racks and well plates | A grid device (a 96-well plate is a status grid), identifier-first type |
| Ingredient and nutrition panels | A ruled panel with weights, a numeric style |
| Colour drawdowns, swatches and spec chips | An accent with a measured source |

## Standards

- **GMP / GLP** and **ALCOA+** (attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate): every record shows who and when.
- **GHS** pictograms and the **SDS** section order.
- **SPC** conventions (Shewhart charts, Western Electric rules) for out-of-control marks.
- Seed-to-sale and recall rules where the sector has them: a lot's whole tree is reachable from one code.

## Archetype pressure

Rows and lists (lots, samples, results), marks (in spec, out of spec, on hold, released, recalled), patterns (a limit chart, a plate map), modals (a signature with a reason).

## The category template

Blue molecules and a DNA helix, hexagon patterns, a scientist in goggles at a pipette, a glowing flask, white and pale blue cards; for food, a splash or a farm-to-table photo; for cannabis, green leaves on black.
