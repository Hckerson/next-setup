# Ledger

Banks, books, budgets and donations. The amount is the content, and the world's rule is that every amount is entered, ruled, balanced and signed by someone.

## Artefacts

| Artefact | Can supply |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Bound columnar ledgers (red and blue ruling, debit and credit columns, carried-forward totals) | Rules as the elevation recipe, numeric alignment, a ruled ground |
| Cheques (security print, guilloche, the MICR line) | A signature texture or numeric face, a line of machine-read figures |
| Bank and card statements (opening balance, entries, closing balance) | Row anatomy (date, description, amount, running balance), totals treatment |
| Adding-machine and till tapes | A narrow mono column, the totalled line, the paper strip as a shape |
| Rubber stamps (PAID, RECEIVED, POSTED, with the date) | Status vocabulary with a physical mark, a rotated or inked device used once |
| Annual reports and impact reports | Display type for a single large figure, the chart as evidence |
| Banknote and stamp engraving | A fine-line accent, a security-print tone |

## Standards

- **ISO 4217** currency codes and minor units; **ISO 13616** IBAN printed in groups of four.
- **E-13B** (the MICR font) as a numeric reference, never as body text.
- Double-entry conventions: credits and debits, brackets or a sign for negatives, the double rule under a total.
- Local plain-language rules for money (fees and rates shown before commitment).

## Archetype pressure

Rows and lists (statements, reconciliations), type (tabular figures, sign, currency placement), marks (posted, pending, failed, reversed), modals (a transfer confirmation with every figure in view).

## The category template

A purple or deep-blue gradient, a 3D credit card floating at an angle, "banking for the modern…", a phone mockup showing a donut chart, glassmorphism cards, a logo cloud of investors. Money shown as an illustration instead of as entries.
